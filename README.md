# NFL Trivia

A standalone, multi-mode NFL trivia game. No installs, no build step — one static app that runs entirely in your browser.

## How to run it

1. Double-click **`index.html`** (or open it from your browser with `File > Open`).
2. That's it. Works offline, on your phone or laptop, in any modern browser.

## Game modes

- **Classic Quiz** — pick a category/difficulty and a round length (5/10/20/30 questions), instant right/wrong feedback. 482 questions: the original 300-question bank from the draft app's Trivia tab, plus 233 more hard questions added later (answer order was re-shuffled on import — the source spreadsheet had the correct answer as option A about 90% of the time), later audited for duplicates/answer-leaks/factual accuracy (see "A note on the trivia content" below).
- **Immaculate Grid** — every round deals a brand-new random 3x3 grid (team/draft/award/position criteria on the rows and columns), drawn from a 1144-player pool spanning 1960s greats through current stars. Type a player who satisfies both the row and the column for that square. One guess per square, no reusing a player twice on the same board, and rarer correct answers score more points — the fewer players in the pool who fit that square, the more it's worth.
- **Blitz** — Sporcle-style: pick a list ("Name all 32 teams," "Super Bowl-winning QBs," etc.), a timer (60/90/120s), and type as many correct answers as you can before time runs out. Nicknames and abbreviations are accepted.
- **Speed Round** — rapid-fire multiple choice, about 6 seconds per question, non-stop for the length of the session (60/90/120s). Chaining correct answers builds a streak multiplier for bonus points.
- **Silhouette** — a random player from a 252-player pool spanning current stars and Hall of Famers. If a real cutout image exists for that player in `assets/silhouettes/` it's shown; otherwise it falls back to a generic football-player pictogram. Wrong guesses (or asking for a hint) reveal a clue — position, draft decade, team(s), accolades (Hall of Fame / MVP / Super Bowl champion status), signature move, then a scouting-style "recognition notes" clue. Fewer clues used = more points.

- **Football IQ Test** — 25 questions across every category, mixed difficulty, no right/wrong feedback until the final screen (like a real test). Your score maps to a Football IQ (60-160) with a title ("Football Genius," "Solid Fan," etc.) and a per-category correct/total breakdown.
- **17-0** — a Sleeper 17-0-style historical fantasy draft. Each of 7 rounds rolls a random notable NFL team-season (1999-2025) from a curated ~160-entry pool; draft one player from that roster into an open slot (1 QB, 2 RB, 2 WR, 1 TE, 1 FLEX). You get one team re-roll and one year re-roll for the whole draft. After 7 picks, real approximate PPR points-per-game plus roster chemistry (past teammates, same college, same draft class, curated Elite/Legendary duos like Brady-Gronk) produce a final score, a letter grade (S down to F, per Sleeper's own scale), and a projected record out of 17 — scaled against a data-derived "perfect team" ceiling.
- **CFB 16-0** — the college football edition, adapted from a user-provided game-design spreadsheet (`CFB_16-0_Game_Template.xlsx`). Same roll → pick → chemistry → grade loop as 17-0, but 8 rounds instead of 7 (adds a DEF slot on top of the usual QB/2RB/2WR/TE/FLEX), drawn from a curated pool of 25 real college team-seasons (1990-2025). DEF is a whole **team defense unit** (e.g. "2011 Alabama Defense"), not an individual defender — standard fantasy-football convention — which means the DEF slot comes from whichever team+year happens to get rolled that round, letting you pair, say, a great defense from one team-year with a QB drafted from an entirely different one. Chemistry adds two CFB-specific bonuses on top of the shared ones — Same School and Same Signing Class — plus a curated Elite/Legendary duo list taken verbatim from the spec. Branded "16-0" rather than "17-0" because the spec's own math for a bye-earning top playoff seed (12 regular-season wins + conference championship + a 3-round CFP run) comes out to 16 games, not 17 — so wins/losses scale off 16. The result screen names the specific playoff round (or bowl game, for rosters that missed the field) and a one-line grade verdict, per the spec's postseason-narrative instructions. See "A note on the CFB 16-0 content" below.
- **College Football Quiz** — same engine as Classic Quiz (category/difficulty filters, 5/10/20/30-question rounds), pointed at its own 456-question CFB bank (Heisman Trophy, National Championships, Rivalries, Coaches, Players & Legends, Records & Stats, Bowls & Playoff, Traditions & Culture, Conferences & History, Deep Cuts). Fully separate from the NFL quiz and leaderboard.
- **College Football IQ Test** — the IQ Test format (25 questions, no feedback until the end, 60-160 score) run against the CFB bank instead of NFL. Separate leaderboard from the NFL IQ Test.
- **CFB Immaculate Grid** — same generative-grid engine as the NFL Grid, drawn from a 938-player consensus All-America pool (1889-2025) across 48 programs. Criteria span school, conference (SEC/Big Ten/ACC/Big 12/Ivy League/Independent), position, era bracket, Heisman/National Champion/Multi-Year All-American/Pro Football Hall of Fame, 17 individual awards (Maxwell, Outland, Lombardi, Walter Camp, Davey O'Brien, Butkus, Jim Thorpe, Johnny Unitas Golden Arm, Doak Walker, Lou Groza, Bronko Nagurski, Chuck Bednarik, Fred Biletnikoff, Rimington, Ray Guy, John Mackey, Ted Hendricks — complete year-by-year winner history, not just recent years), and NFL first-round draft pick status.

The Immaculate Grid's row/column team headers also show a small circular badge in that team's real colors with its abbreviation — original artwork, not the actual NFL logos (no logo image assets are bundled with this app).

Every mode ends with your result saved to a shared **live leaderboard** (see below), plus your personal best is remembered locally so it survives even offline.

## Football Rating

The first time you enter a name, the app walks you straight into a 16-question intro test (a blend of NFL and College Football questions) before you reach Home. Your result sets a starting **Football Rating**, shown as a small badge (e.g. "🏈 132") in the header from then on.

From there, the rating isn't static — every game mode you finish (13 regular modes, plus the Daily Challenge) nudges it up or down a little based on how well you did relative to your current rating, using a slow-moving average (`RATING_DRIFT_ALPHA = 0.08` in `app.js`, so any single round only pulls the rating ~8% of the way toward that session's result). One bad or lucky round barely moves it; the number instead settles toward your real skill level over many sessions.

The rating is stored per name (`localStorage`, keyed off a slugified version of your name) so switching names starts fresh. You can retake the intro test anytime from the "🔄 Retake Intro Test" button on the Home screen, which resets the rating back to a new baseline.

**It also syncs across devices**, unlike every other mode's leaderboard entry. Every other mode's Firestore doc is keyed `name+deviceId`, so the same name played on two devices shows up as two separate leaderboard rows — that's fine for a leaderboard, but wrong for a single persistent rating. The rating doc instead is keyed by name only (`rating__<slug>`), so it's one canonical value shared across every device. Whenever fresh leaderboard data arrives (`reconcileRating()` in `app.js`), whichever device has the higher `games` count — i.e. the more "lived-in" version — wins and propagates to the other device(s). If a name switches to a brand-new device with no local rating yet but a cloud one already exists, it adopts the cloud value automatically (and cancels the intro test if it had just started one). Shows up as its own "Football Rating" tab on the shared leaderboard too. This only works when the app is served over http(s) with Firebase connected, same caveat as the rest of the shared-sync system — over `file://` it's local-only, same as before.

## Splash screen

`index.html` has a `#splash-screen` overlay (Reads logo + welcome message) that shows immediately on load and fades out after ~1.3s regardless of whether Firebase has connected yet — it's a purely cosmetic branded loading moment, not a gate on anything, so a slow or broken connection never leaves anyone stuck looking at it.

## Sound

`sound.js` handles background music and sound effects — see `assets/audio/EXPECTED_FILENAMES.txt` for the full current mapping. A few things worth knowing:

- **Background music is a shuffled playlist**, not one looping file — 19 real marching band tracks (13 of them public-domain John Philip Sousa marches), auto-advancing with no back-to-back repeats, playing only while the Home screen is showing.
- **It starts on the first tap/click/keypress anywhere on the page**, as early as browsers allow — audio-with-sound can't legally autoplay before a real user gesture in any browser, so true page-load autoplay isn't possible, but a dedicated one-time listener (`sound.js`) catches the very first interaction and starts it immediately rather than waiting for a specific button.
- **It stops the instant the tab/app is backgrounded** — phone screen locked, app switched away from, browser minimized, or the tab loses focus on desktop. All of those trigger the Page Visibility API the same way (`document.hidden`), so one `visibilitychange` listener covers every case. Resumes automatically when you come back, if you're still on Home.
- **Correct/wrong/complete effects** are real audio files (crowd cheer / referee whistle / stadium chant) that fire across every game mode. Only one plays at a time — starting a new one, or advancing to the next question, always cuts off whatever's still playing (`stopSfx()`), so a long cheer/whistle clip never bleeds into the next question.
- **The wrong-answer whistle is trimmed to one blast in code** (`SFX_MAX_DURATION` in `sound.js`, ~650ms) — the source file is a longer clip than needed for a quick "wrong" cue, and there's no audio-editing tooling in this environment to trim the file itself, so playback is just cut short instead.
- **A crowd-boo effect plays instead of the completion chant on a bad result** — 60% or fewer correct for the percentage-scored modes (Quiz, CFB Quiz, Speed, CFB Speed, Blitz, CFB Blitz, Silhouette, Daily Challenge), a Football IQ / CFB IQ score of 60 or below (the absolute floor of that 60-160 scale — effectively "got everything wrong"), or an F grade in 17-0 / CFB 16-0. Plays regardless of Practice-vs-Ranked status, same as the normal completion sound it replaces. Grid/CFB Grid are unaffected — their per-square scoring doesn't map onto a "60%" threshold the same way.
- **Click sound** plays on every button press, including every answer button. It tries `assets/audio/click.mp3` first; if that file isn't there, it synthesizes a short click tone with the Web Audio API instead — so this one always works with zero files required. It fires ~90ms before the correct/wrong/complete effect on the same tap so the click doesn't get buried under a louder, longer clip starting in the same instant.
- Missing files anywhere just stay silent, no errors — same graceful-fallback philosophy as the Silhouette photos.
- A 🔊/🔇 toggle in the header mutes everything at once.
- The IQ Test, CFB IQ Test, and the intro test deliberately don't play correct/wrong sounds per question, since those modes hide feedback until the final screen by design — a sound cue would give the answer away.

## Navigation

The top nav is 4 buttons (Home / NFL ▾ / CFB ▾ / Leaderboard), not one per mode. Clicking NFL or CFB opens a picker panel listing that league's modes — a small anchored dropdown on desktop/tablet, a full-width slide-up bottom sheet on mobile (same markup, different CSS per breakpoint). Under 640px width, the top nav disappears entirely in favor of a fixed bottom tab bar (Home / NFL / CFB / Board) — the standard mobile game-app pattern — and the footer hides since the bottom nav is the persistent chrome there instead. The mode list lives in one place (`LEAGUE_MODES` in `app.js`) that both the Home screen's card grid and the picker panel read from, so they can't drift out of sync — add a mode to `LEAGUE_MODES` and it shows up in both automatically.

## Onboarding & visual design

- A short **3-step** dismissible walkthrough (what Reads is + the Football Rating concept → a quick visual overview of the mode types → how the rating/leaderboard sync across devices) auto-opens once, the very first time the app loads on a device (`nflTriviaOnboarded` in localStorage), and is reopenable any time via the "❓" button in the header. It's a centered modal, not a blocking gate — Skip/Escape/backdrop-click all dismiss it — and follows the same accessible-dialog pattern as the NFL/CFB mode picker (focus moves in on open, returns to the trigger on close). The final step's button is a real call-to-action, not a generic "close" — it reads "Start the Intro Test" for a brand-new visitor (landing back on Home with the name field focused, or starting the test directly if a name's already saved) or "Try Today's Challenge" for anyone who already has a rating (jumps straight to the Daily Challenge). Steps live in `ONBOARDING_STEPS`, the CTA logic in `onboardingFinish()`, both in `app.js`.
- The home hero has a bold tagline (`.hero-tagline`) plus a supporting line about the Football Rating, instead of one flat sentence.
- The flagship mode in each league (Quiz, Grid, and IQ Test) gets a visually heavier `.mode-card.featured` treatment on the Home screen — bigger icon/title, a subtle accent-tinted border and gradient — so new users have an obvious "start here" instead of 12 identical-weight buttons. Controlled by a `featured: true` flag per entry in `LEAGUE_MODES`.
- The full-bleed logo watermark behind every screen was toned down (opacity 0.18/0.12 → 0.09/0.06, and a smaller max size) — it was reducing readability more than it needed to for a background flourish.
- Answer feedback gets a small, deliberate animation instead of an instant color swap: quiz options and grid squares pop-and-glow on a correct answer and shake-and-flash on a wrong one (`pop-feedback-strong`/`shake-feedback-strong` keyframes — a stronger variant of the shared `pop-feedback`/`shake-feedback` used for smaller secondary feedback like Blitz's matched-answer chips and the Silhouette reveal). These are plain CSS animations tied to the `.correct`/`.wrong` classes — since `renderAll()` rebuilds each screen from an HTML string rather than mutating existing nodes, every one of those elements is a fresh DOM node each time the class appears, so the animation just replays automatically with no JS trigger needed. The header's Football Rating badge is the one exception (it's a persistent node whose text gets mutated in place), so it explicitly toggles a `.rating-pulse` class with a forced reflow whenever the shown score actually changes value. All of this respects `prefers-reduced-motion` like everything else in the app.
- Score/rating displays (`.iq-score`, `.legends-grade`, `.summary-score`, `#rating-badge`, and the pre-existing `.blitz-timer`) use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` so digits don't shift width as they change, plus slightly tightened letter-spacing on the two largest ones.
- Switching into a lazy-loaded mode shows a real loading state (`.loading-panel` — spinner + "Loading NFL Grid…"-style copy naming the actual mode, via `modeLabelFor()`) instead of a bare "Loading…" string.
- The Daily Challenge card (`.daily-card`) is visually elevated — heavier border, a soft accent glow, bigger title, and a full-width primary button — so it reads as the most prominent thing on Home rather than just another card.

## Profile, streak, and badges

A "📊 Stats" button next to "Change" in the name bar (shown once a name is set) opens a Profile screen — Football Rating (with a small inline SVG sparkline of its last 20 values, hand-rolled with no charting library — `ratingSparklineSvg()`/`RATING_HISTORY_MAX = 20`, local-only, not synced to Firebase, appended every time `updateRatingDrift()` actually moves the score plus the one-time intro-test seed), current streak, an achievement-badge grid, and a card per mode reusing `LEADERBOARD_MODES`' own label/column config against `state.stats` instead of the Firebase-sourced leaderboard data. `state.stats` has been updated by every mode's finish function since day one but had no reader until this — the Profile screen is its first UI. The streak itself is local-only (not synced to Firebase) and is tied specifically to completing the Daily Challenge (see below), not to playing any mode — `bumpStreak()`/`getStreak()` in `app.js`.

**Badges** (`BADGES` array in `app.js`) are computed live every time Profile renders — no separate "earned" data to keep in sync, just a threshold check against stats every mode already tracks (e.g. Perfect Grid = a clean-swept Immaculate Grid, Blitz Master = 20+ matched in one Blitz round, CFB Specialist = 20+ rounds played across every CFB mode combined, On Fire = a 7-day Daily Challenge streak). Locked badges show a 🔒 and dimmed styling; earned ones get their real icon and an accent-tinted card.

**Continue where you left off**: `enterMode()` records the last real game mode entered (`nflTriviaLastMode` in localStorage — deliberately only actual modes from `LEAGUE_MODES`, not navigational screens like Leaderboard/Profile/Daily), and Home shows a "Continue where you left off" card pointing straight back at it (`continuePlayingCardHtml()`) whenever one exists.

## Practice vs Ranked

Every one of the 12 existing game modes (not Daily Challenge — see below) has a "🎯 Ranked" / "🎓 Practice" chip-row on its "about to start" screen (`rankedToggleHtml(mode)`, reusing the existing `chip-toggle` component). Ranked is the default and behaves exactly as before; switching to Practice for a round skips that round's stats update, rating drift, and leaderboard push (the round still plays normally, still makes sound, you just won't see it counted anywhere afterward) — a small "🎓 Practice — this round won't be saved" pill shows in the toolbar for the whole round so it's never ambiguous. Each mode remembers its own last choice independently (`state.rankedPref`, `nflTriviaRankedPref` in localStorage). Implemented as one shared toggle/pill/preference mechanism reused identically across all 12 modes' start/finish functions, rather than a bespoke one per mode.

## Daily Challenge

A prominent card at the top of the Home screen (`.daily-card`) — the one deliberate exception to "every round is freshly randomized." It's a fixed 10-question set (5 NFL + 5 CFB, same blend as the intro test) that's identical for every player on a given calendar date: a tiny seeded PRNG (`mulberry32`, seeded from a hash of `YYYY-MM-DD`) shuffles the shared `QUIZ`/`CFB` pools deterministically instead of using `Math.random()`, so "today's challenge" really is the same question set for everyone, no new data authoring needed. One attempt per day (tracked per-name under `nflTriviaDaily__<slug>`); it always counts toward the Football Rating and leaderboard — the only mode with no Practice option, since a Daily Challenge that doesn't count isn't really a daily challenge. Completing it advances the [streak](#profile--streak).

## Sharing results

Every mode's result screen has a "📤 Share" button (`shareResultCard(mode)` in `app.js`). It renders a branded 1080×1080 result card on an offscreen `<canvas>` (text/shapes only, no image assets drawn onto it — so this works even over `file://`, unlike Firebase sync or the service worker) and hands it off one of two ways:

- **On devices with the Web Share API** (mostly mobile Safari/Chrome), it calls `navigator.share()` directly with the image file — this opens the real native OS share sheet, which is the *only* way to get a working Instagram target, since Instagram has no web share-intent of its own (it only accepts images via the native share sheet, never text or links from a website).
- **Everywhere else** (desktop, unsupported browsers), a small modal opens instead — matching the same accessible-dialog pattern as the onboarding modal — with a preview of the card, a Download button, a "Share to X" button (X/Twitter has a real web intent that accepts prefilled text), a "Copy Text Summary" button (`navigator.clipboard.writeText` where available — needs http(s)/localhost, same as the rest of the clipboard API — falling back to the older `execCommand('copy')`-via-hidden-textarea trick everywhere else, including `file://`), and, only when served over http(s), a "Share App to Facebook" button. Facebook's own web sharer only accepts a URL, not custom text or images — it's a deliberate Meta anti-spam restriction, not something any app can work around — so that button honestly shares a link to the app (using the existing OG meta tags) rather than pretending to post the personalized score image.
- Every card and share text also includes **how much that round moved your Football Rating** ("🏈 Rating +3 (now 128)"), when applicable — omitted entirely for Practice-mode rounds or before you have a rating yet. `updateRatingDrift()` tracks this in a shared `lastRatingDelta` variable, and each mode's finish function stashes it onto that round's own state (`state.quiz.ratingDelta`, etc.) right after calling it, so `shareConfigFor()` can read it back later without recomputing anything.

## Performance: lazy-loaded data files

Only `data/quiz.js` and `data/cfb.js` load eagerly (static `<script>` tags in `index.html`) — they're needed immediately by the intro test every brand-new player takes, plus the NFL Quiz/Speed/IQ modes that already reuse the same pools. The other 7 data files (`grid.js`, `blitz.js`, `silhouette.js`, `legends.js`+`legends-meta.js`, `cfb-blitz.js`, `cfb-grid.js`, and `cfb-speed.js` — the single biggest one, ~40% of all data by itself) load on first entry into the mode that actually needs them, via a small script-injecting loader (`loadScript`/`MODE_DATA_FILES`/`goToMode` in `app.js`). `goToMode` shows a brief "Loading…" panel while a mode's files fetch, then proceeds normally — a one-time cost the first time you visit that mode per page load, never again after. If a load fails (offline, bad connection), it shows a retry-friendly error instead of leaving the screen stuck.

## Accessibility & PWA

- Every clickable element in this app is a real `<button>` (never a `<div>` with a click handler), so keyboard operability — Tab to focus, Enter/Space to activate — already works natively throughout, including quiz options and grid cells. `:focus-visible` gets a visible accent-colored outline matching the app's look instead of the browser default.
- The NFL/CFB mode picker follows the standard accessible-dialog pattern: opening it moves focus into the panel, closing it (✕, backdrop click, or Escape) returns focus to whichever button opened it, and `aria-expanded`/`aria-haspopup` stay in sync with its open/closed state.
- Navigating between modes moves focus to the new screen's content (screen-reader users get announced into it, since replacing `#app`'s HTML would otherwise silently drop focus to `<body>`) — but only on an actual screen change, not on every re-render within the same mode, so it doesn't interrupt typing or answering questions.
- `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` collapses all animations/transitions to near-instant for anyone with that OS setting on.
- Fixed two real WCAG AA contrast failures: white text on the accent-orange background (`.btn-primary`, active nav/chip states) measured ~2.9:1 against the ~4.5:1 minimum — a dedicated always-dark `--accent-text` color fixes this everywhere it's used, in both themes. The accent orange itself, used as text/icon color in a few places (IQ score, 17-0 grade, bottom-nav active state), measured ~2.9:1 against light-theme's white cards — light theme now uses a deeper shade of the same hue (~4.8:1) instead, dark theme unchanged. The Immaculate Grid's per-team colored badges (32 NFL + 48 CFB, each a different real color) now pick whichever of white/near-black text actually contrasts better against that specific color, rather than assuming white always works.
- `manifest.json` + `sw.js` make this a real installable PWA when served over http(s) — the service worker is network-first (not cache-first), since this app's content files get updated fairly often and a stale cache with no update path would be worse than no offline support at all. Requires http(s)/localhost (browsers block service workers on `file://`), so it silently no-ops for local file use — same documented limitation as the shared leaderboard.
- Open Graph / Twitter Card meta tags are in the static `index.html` head, so link previews (Slack, Discord, X, iMessage, etc.) work without needing server-side rendering — those crawlers read the raw HTML head directly, they don't execute the JS app.

## Live sync (Firebase) & deploying to Netlify

This app reuses the same Firebase project as the draft app, under its own collection (`games/nflTrivia/leaderboard`) so scores never mix with the draft app's leaderboard.

- **Local file (`file://`) use still works** for solo play — every mode is fully playable offline — but browsers block ES-module script loading over `file://`, so the sync dot (top right) will just stay disconnected and scores won't share across devices.
- **The real shared leaderboard needs the app served over http(s)** — drag-and-drop the whole `nfl-trivia` folder (not just `index.html`) into Netlify. All the files need to travel together.

## Updating content later

- **Add/edit quiz questions**: `data/quiz.js` is `window.QUIZ_DATA = [ { id, category, difficulty, question, options: [4 strings], correctIndex (0-3), notes } ]`. Hand Claude a new question spreadsheet and ask it to regenerate this file, same as the draft app's `trivia.js`.
- **Add Immaculate Grid players**: `data/grid.js` — `window.GRID_PLAYERS` is a flat array of player objects (teams played for, position, draft round/year, college, awards). Add more players to make grids richer and less repetitive; the criteria library (`window.GRID_CRITERIA`) auto-generates every grid by sampling row/column filters and checking there's at least one valid answer per square, so new players slot in automatically — no other code changes needed. Team colors for the grid badges live in `window.GRID_TEAM_COLORS` in the same file.
- **Add Silhouette players**: `data/silhouette.js` — `window.SILHOUETTE_PLAYERS` is a flat array of `{ name, position, team, pose, notes, difficulty, draftDecade, hof, mvp, sbChamp }`. `notes` is the strongest clue (a short scouting-style description of what makes that player recognizable), so write it the way you'd describe someone from their outline/movement alone, not their stats. `hof`/`mvp`/`sbChamp` are booleans that drive the "Accolades" clue — for 221 of the 252 players these were cross-referenced directly from `data/grid.js`'s existing `GRID_PLAYERS` accolade data (name-matched, suffix-normalized) rather than re-researched from scratch; the other 31 (mostly pre-Super Bowl-era Hall of Famers not in the Grid pool) were filled in individually.
- **Add real per-player silhouette images**: drop a PNG into `assets/silhouettes/` named exactly like `assets/silhouettes/EXPECTED_FILENAMES.txt` lists (lowercase, spaces/punctuation → hyphens — e.g. "Patrick Mahomes" → `patrick-mahomes.png`). No code changes needed — the game always tries to load that file first and only falls back to the generic pictogram if it's missing. See "Adding real player silhouettes" below for how to make the actual cutouts.
- **Add Blitz lists**: `data/blitz.js` — `window.BLITZ_LISTS` is an array of `{ id, title, prompt, answers: [{ answer, aliases }] }`. Add a new list object and it shows up automatically on the Blitz picker screen.
- **Add 17-0 team-seasons**: `data/legends.js` — `window.LEGENDS_TEAMS` is an array of `{ team, year, players: [{ name, position, fppg }] }`. Add more team-seasons to make rolls feel less repetitive; the draft engine automatically re-rolls until it lands on an entry with at least one legal pick for your open slots, so new entries slot in with no other code changes. `data/legends-meta.js` — `window.PLAYER_META` (draft year + college per player, for chemistry) and `window.LEGENDS_DUOS` (curated Elite/Legendary connection pairs) — add an entry here for any new player you add to `legends.js` or their chemistry just won't trigger (harmless, just less rich).
- **Add CFB questions**: `data/cfb.js` is `window.CFB_DATA`, same shape as `data/quiz.js`. Both College Football Quiz and College Football IQ Test read from this one file.

## Adding real player silhouettes

The game can't generate these itself — cutting a player cleanly out of a photo needs actual image editing (background/subject removal), which isn't something scriptable. Here's the workflow, using your original spreadsheets' own instructions:

1. **Find a photo.** Each spreadsheet row already had a "Best Google Image Search Query" — use that in Google/Bing Images. Prefer a clear side or 3/4 action shot with the full body visible (throwing, running, rushing, contested catch).
2. **Cut the player out.** **remove.bg** — upload the photo, it auto-removes the background in one click and gives you a transparent-background PNG. That's the only editing step needed: the app itself crushes every photo to a flat black silhouette on load (CSS `filter: brightness(0)` on `.silhouette-photo` in `styles.css`), so you don't need to manually threshold/blacken it in Photopea or anything else — jersey colors, numbers, and logos all get hidden automatically no matter what the source photo looks like.
3. **Export as a transparent PNG** and name it exactly per `assets/silhouettes/EXPECTED_FILENAMES.txt` (one line per player: `Player Name -> expected-filename.png`).
4. **Drop the PNG into `assets/silhouettes/`.** Refresh the app — that player now shows the real silhouette in Silhouette mode automatically, no code changes.

You don't have to do all 252 at once — do a batch of your favorites/most iconic players first; everyone else keeps using the generic pictogram fallback until you get to them.

## A note on the trivia content

The Classic Quiz bank is carried over verbatim from the draft app. The Immaculate Grid player pool and Blitz answer lists were authored from general NFL knowledge rather than pulled from any specific source, and were spot-checked for accuracy during development — but with this much sports trivia, it's worth flagging anything that looks off so it can get fixed.

A later full audit of `data/quiz.js` (533 → 482 questions) removed: 47 duplicate/near-duplicate questions (the same fact asked twice with reworded phrasing, e.g. two separate questions both asking who set the 1984 single-season sack record), 2 questions with no defensible single correct answer (real historical ties — e.g. "who returned the most INTs for TDs in a season" has multiple players tied at 4, so "Multiple with 4" was the only honest answer, which doesn't work as multiple choice), and 1 question whose claimed fact was directly contradicted by another question elsewhere in the same file (a "Peyton Manning is the only player to win Super Bowl MVP with two different franchises" question, when Tom Brady also did this with Tampa Bay in 2021). Also fixed: 6 questions that gave away their own answer (e.g. "The Madden NFL video game is named after which coach?" when only one of the 4 options contained the word "Madden"; a Houston Oilers question that incorrectly attributed the team's name to Ohio oil refineries instead of the Texas oil industry), and 2 questions where the "correct" option was a vague non-answer ("Both" / "All contributed") instead of a specific name, reworded to point at the actual specific answer.

## A note on the 17-0 content

The user's own roster-links spreadsheet (`NFL_Roster_Links_1999_to_2026.xlsx`) is 893 links to Pro-Football-Reference pages, not the actual stats — scraping all of those wasn't feasible here. `data/legends.js` and `data/legends-meta.js` are instead a curated ~160-entry pool (about 5 seasons per franchise) authored from known real stat lines and career facts, with fantasy points-per-game estimated as season total ÷ 16 games. Treat the numbers as "in the right neighborhood," not box-score exact — same tolerance as the rest of this app's content. The grade-scale bands (S/A+/A/.../F) and the record-out-of-17 are this app's own design (the official Sleeper 17-0 template explicitly says there's no public FPPG→grade formula), scaled against a perfect-team ceiling computed from the best FPPG at each position in the pool.

## A note on the CFB 16-0 content

Built from a user-provided spreadsheet (`CFB_16-0_Game_Template-2.xlsx`) that was written as a spec for an interactive, AI-narrated session (live ESPN lookups each round, freeform postseason narration) rather than this app's static/offline architecture — so the *rules* were ported faithfully but the *process* was translated into the same baked-data pattern the NFL 17-0 mode already uses.

`data/cfb-legends.js` is sourced directly from that spreadsheet's "Featured Team Rosters" sheet: **207 real team-seasons across 69 FBS programs** (1990-2025, every Power 4 team, all 16 SEC teams, plus Notre Dame and Boise State), each with a real QB, RB, and two more skill weapons (WR or TE, occasionally a 2nd RB) plus a team DEFENSE unit. Unlike the sheet's own defense section (written for individual-defender stats, since it was adapted from the NFL 17-0 template which has no defense concept to begin with), **DEF here is a whole team unit** (e.g. `"2011 Alabama Defense"`) — real fantasy football always scores DEF/ST as one team, never a specific player, and a team-unit DEF is also the only way to let a rolled team's defense get drafted independently of whichever team its offense came from. DEF FPPG is derived from each team's real season points-allowed figure (given in the sheet) run through the standard fantasy points-allowed bracket, plus a modeled bonus for likely sacks/turnovers/special-teams scoring (the sheet itself says the full box score isn't available, so this is a transparent stand-in, not a fabricated exact count). The sheet lists names and highlights but not individual stat lines, so offensive FPPG is an honest approximation — a per-position baseline with a small per-player spread, a bump for national-championship seasons, and a bump specifically for that season's actual Heisman winner (detected from the sheet's own highlight tags, not runner-ups). Same "approximate, not box-score exact" tolerance as the rest of this app's content.

The result screen also implements the spec's full postseason-narrative instruction, not just a bare grade: S-through-A- rosters are named National Champions; B+/B/B- rosters are told the exact Playoff round they lost in (National Championship Game / Quarterfinal / First Round, matching each grade's own "Runner-Up"/"2nd Round Exit"/"First Round Exit" label); C+ and below are told they missed the 12-team field and named a real, grade-appropriate bowl game instead (`CFB_BOWL_NAMES` in `app.js`, picked randomly per playthrough for variety), per the spec's "BOWL GAME FALLBACK" note. A one-line grade verdict (`cfbLegendsVerdict()`) accompanies the grade too, matching the spec's "fun one-line verdict" instruction (e.g. "Iconic 👑 — this squad is a problem.").

`data/cfb-legends-meta.js`'s curated Elite/Legendary duo list is taken verbatim from the spreadsheet's own chemistry-reference sheet, not invented — a few of those named pairs use players outside the current pool, so those specific bonuses are currently unreachable until the pool grows to include them.

## A note on the Immaculate Grid and CFB content

The 1144-player Grid pool was built by merging four of the user's own files: `NFL_HighValue_Players_ImmaculateGrid` (888 structured players, historical + notable current), `2026_1-500_Fantasy_Football_Rankings.xlsx` and `NFL_Active_Players_ImmaculateGrid` (424 players with full career team/college/draft data, used to fill in current-roster depth the other files were missing) — merged on top of the original hand-authored 118.
